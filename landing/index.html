A safety crew for AI coding agents

Your AI agent gets the keys to the room, not the house.

Tini reads your prompt and builds a fence around only what the agent needs. Tina checks everything that comes in and everything that goes out. The agent builds inside the yard, and nothing leaves until every part of the fence is green.

The problem

Coding agents can reach your whole computer.

An agent like Claude Code runs on your laptop with your permissions. Asked to build a small website, it can open your photos, your documents and your keys. It asks before it acts, but after the fortieth "Yes" nobody is reading anymore.

What can go wrong

  • Over-access. The agent can read far more than the job needs: a whole Pictures folder, HR files, credentials.
  • Leaks at launch. What it builds can ship an API key in page code, or photos that still carry GPS and the owner's name.
  • Hidden instructions. A downloaded template can tell the agent to read your SSH key.

The protections already exist

Claude Code ships permission rules, hooks and an operating-system sandbox. But by default the sandbox lets commands read the entire computer, ~/.ssh included, and almost nobody writes these rules by hand for every task.

The sandbox exists. Nobody configures it. Tini writes the borders for you, from what you asked for.

How it works

Least privilege, written from your prompt.

You type what you want, the way you'd give a work order. The family takes it from there.

1Tini builds the fence from your prompt

Code finds every folder your prompt names and checks each one: it must exist, sit in your home folder, and not be a private location. Tina pre-inspects those folders. You get one plain-English card: what the agent can use, what gets stripped (like GPS locations in photos), and "everything else on your Mac stays outside." One Approve replaces dozens of unread Yes clicks.

2Three layers hold the fence

  • Staged copies. The agent works in a fresh project folder. Tini copies in only the approved files, with photo locations removed. Your real folders are never opened by the agent.
  • A check on every action. Every read, write, search and command goes through a hook. Anything outside the workspace, or any website not on the list, is denied on the spot with a plain reason.
  • The OS sandbox. macOS's own sandbox backs the hook up, so a shell command can't read what the file tools can't.

When the agent needs more, it asks. Tina inspects the folder first, and you pick the narrowest safe option, like "only the 12 job-site photos, locations removed."

3Tina inspects before and after every turn

Before folders come in, she counts GPS-tagged photos and finds private documents by their content, like a driver's license scan hidden among 1,212 files. After every turn, she scans what the agent wrote: secrets with gitleaks, leftover photo metadata, personal data and risky files. Each problem turns the segment it came from red, with a plain explanation and a one-click fix.

4Nothing leaves until every segment is green

Launch stays locked while any segment is red. Fix the problems and it opens. Ask for another change and it locks again until Tina has checked the new work. At the end you get an access report: what was allowed, blocked, narrowed and fixed, turn by turn, and which outside servers the site talks to.

Privacy by design

AI never decides access.

Checking your private photos by uploading them to a cloud AI would be the leak itself. So the safety decisions are plain code, and the AI only ever sees summaries.

Plain code, on your Mac
  • Finds the folders in your prompt and decides what's inside the fence
  • Copies files and strips photo locations
  • Checks every action the agent takes
  • Scans for secrets, metadata and personal data
  • Applies the fixes and rechecks
The AI helper, on summaries only
  • Writes the labels and plain-English explanations
  • Suggests which files in a folder match your request, from names and counts
  • Sees folder names, file counts and redacted snippets, never your files or secret values
  • Every answer is checked by code, and it can never add a file or grant access

If the AI is down, saved answers and built-in wording take over. The fence doesn't depend on it.
